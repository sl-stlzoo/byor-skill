How to read this radar

8 blips across four quadrants. The rings run from the centre outwards.

  • Adopt 3

    Proven here and mature. Not using it, where it fits, would be a poor and slightly irresponsible choice.

  • Trial 2

    Used for real work, in a limited way. We want to learn whether it earns a place in the toolkit.

  • Assess 2

    Interesting and worth a closer look. We are learning, not yet relying on it.

  • Caution 1

    We have had a negative experience, or see real risk. Consider alternatives and avoid starting new work with it.

Keeping up without being overwhelmed

  1. Start with Adopt. Are you using each one where it applies? If not, ask why.
  2. Read Caution carefully. If you rely on any of these, plan to reflect and consider moving away.
  3. Then Trial. Learn about the ones relevant to you and see what a trial would take.
  4. Assess last. Worth knowing about, but lower in priority unless it fits your situation perfectly.

Themes for this edition

Patterns we noticed across the blips we discussed. They form the story of this radar.

Fewer tools, clearer owners

Across the interviews the same pattern kept appearing: we pay for several tools that overlap. Zendesk is used by two people while Microsoft 365 already offers a helpdesk, so we now advise against starting anything new in it.

The tools we trust most all have a named owner and a tested backup, which is why QuickBooks and Neon One sit in Adopt. We expect to apply that same bar to every Trial item before the next review.

Two open source options, CiviCRM and Odoo POS, moved onto the radar this edition. Neither is proven here yet, but both could reduce licence cost, so we will watch hosting and support effort closely.

FAQ

What is this radar?

A snapshot of software at Example Community Museum, grouped into four quadrants and four rings. It records what we have actually experienced. It is not a market survey and makes no attempt to cover every product.

Is it a list of approved software?

No. It is a shared view that helps people prioritise learning and decisions. Settled tools we are happy with may be missing because nothing about them is changing.

How are the rings decided?

By discussion and evidence. A blip reaches Trial only with real use on real work. Adopt is reserved for things where not using them, in the right context, would be a poor choice. Caution means we have had a negative experience.

What do position and shape mean?

Quadrants are just topic areas; a blip’s angle inside its quadrant means nothing. Distance from the centre inside a ring does matter: closer to the inner edge means more confidence. Triangles are new this edition, diamonds moved ring, circles are unchanged.

Why is a tool I know missing?

No one nominated it, it did not change since last time, it was cut to keep the radar readable, or we covered a more general idea instead.

How do blips change?

Blips move between rings as our experience grows, and may be dropped after an edition if nothing new happened. Older editions stay in our archive and are not updated.

How do I suggest something?

Email the IT lead with the tool, what you used it for and how it went.

How often is it updated?

Twice a year. Next review: May 2027.
