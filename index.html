Open source · works with any capable AI assistant

Build your own technology radar, by conversation

Paste one prompt into your AI assistant. It interviews you, one question at a time, about your software (ERP, CRM, HR, security, cloud, AI tools and more), places each tool on a Thoughtworks-style radar, and hands you a data file for BYOR plus a polished single-page website.

1. Copy this prompt



Read the agent instructions




My assistant can’t open links
Download BUNDLE.md (every instruction file for building your radar, in one document; BUNDLE-FULL.md adds scripts and consortium material), attach it to the chat, and say: “Follow AGENTS.md inside this file and guide me through building my own technology radar, one question at a time.”


Resume later, or use a customised fork
Say: “Resume my radar using this repo” and paste your saved radar-state.json. To use your own domain list, fork the repo, edit config/domains.yaml, and give the assistant your fork’s link instead.



2. What to expect


    

  1. Setup (about 10 min). Pick quick, standard or deep; describe your organisation; confirm which of the 25 software areas apply; define what Adopt, Trial, Assess and Caution mean for you.
    2. 

  2. Interviews (about 5 min per area at standard depth). One area at a time, each quadrant in turn. You list what you use, pilot and avoid; the assistant proposes a ring with a reason and you confirm.
    3. 

  3. Calibration. You review each ring across the whole radar.
    4. 

  4. Narrative. You approve a tagline, intro, three or four themes and a short action list.
    5. 

  5. Check and publish. The assistant validates the data and walks you through hosting.
    6. 



You can stop at any time and resume. Typical total: about an hour at quick depth, about two at standard.



3. What you get


    

  • index.html: a single-page website with an interactive radar, ring guide, themes, every blip’s write-up, actions and FAQ. Works offline, on phones and in print.
    • 

  • radar.csv / radar.json: load into Thoughtworks Build Your Own Radar.
    • 

  • radar-summary.md and radar-state.json (your editable master record for next edition).


Assistants that cannot save files print the CSV and state in the chat instead, and the site can be hand-built as a lighter alternative.



4. Good to know


  • Security: read SECURITY.md before using it at work. Short version: no accounts, no telemetry, no secrets; the risk is what you tell an AI assistant and where you publish the result.
  • Privacy: the site and CSV list your whole tool stack. Host privately unless you want it public; the site is set to noindex by default.
    • 

  • Custom quadrant names show correctly only on a self-hosted BYOR (one Docker command). The hosted Thoughtworks site needs a compatibility export.
    • 

  • It is your opinion, not a survey. Example tool names in the question lists may be out of date; the assistant should verify them if you allow web lookups.



Benchmark with peer organisations (optional)


A convener can run an informal consortium round: members share a small redacted file (tools, rings, evidence; never descriptions or incidents), everything is processed offline, nothing is shown unless at least three organisations contributed, and everyone gets a benchmark website plus a private “you vs the round” report. See docs/CONSORTIUM.md.
